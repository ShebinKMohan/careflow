- **Room restrictions:** resus never takes a level 3 or 5, and a busy room refuses a call.
- **One recall, then a no-show:** 119 s is still inside the window; at 120 s the token goes to the back of its level; a second miss makes it a no-show and frees the room.
- **Arrival and closing:** arriving in time stops the grace clock, and closing frees the room.
- **Invalid transitions** are refused.

**`test_queue_service.py`** (8, on Postgres):

- **Numbering and order.** Codes A-001, G-001, A-002, A-003; calls come by level and then arrival (A-002 to Resus 1, then A-001 to ED 1, A-003 to ED 2), and each call publishes `token.called`. A session keeps its active token.
- **Concurrency.** 20 concurrent `assign_token` calls give A-001 … A-020, all unique. Mutation check: with the advisory lock and `FOR UPDATE` removed, this test fails.
- **Room restrictions.** Resus refuses a level 3, and a busy room raises.
- **One recall, then a no-show.** The events are `token.called`, `token.called`, `token.no_show`, and the row ends as `no_show` with `recall_count` 1.
- **The nurse's actions:** miss, re-queue behind the next token, arrived, closed; invalid actions and unknown tokens are refused.
- **The wait estimate** on assignment: 0, 10 and 15 minutes.
- **Numbering wraps** after 999 and skips a code an active token still holds (A-999, then A-001).

The model is `QueueCore` itself in a seeded `heapq` loop (`random.Random(42)`, standard library only):

- 8 h of Poisson arrivals at 200/h: 1,589 patients;
- level mix 2/13/40/35/10%;
- exponential service with means of 30/25/20/12/8 min;
- 66 rooms, the first 2 taking levels 1–2 only;
- 5% of calls are no-shows.

Patients who arrive within the horizon are followed until they are seen.

## 3. The board shows only `token_code`, `room` and `status`

`test_the_board_shows_only_token_code_room_and_status`:

- The response is exactly `[{"token_code": "A-002", "room": "Resus 1", "status": "called"}, {"token_code": "A-001", "room": null, "status": "waiting"}]`.
- Every entry's keys must equal the contract `BoardEntry` properties `{token_code, room, status}`, and the contract must keep `additionalProperties: false`. Any other field (a name, a level, a session id) fails the test.
- The route's response model is the contract's `BoardEntry` (`extra="forbid"`).
- `test_only_the_contract_roles_may_call`: only the `board` role reads the board (every other role gets 403, and no token gets 401). The four queue actions are nurse-only.

## Deviations from the issue text

- **Statuses** are the contract's `waiting | called | arrived | no_show | closed`: the issue's `in_room` is `arrived` and `done` is `closed`.
- **Contract additions:** `POST /v1/queue/tokens/{token_id}/close`, so a room can be free again; an optional `room` query parameter on call-next; 404/409 responses. Without a room, call-next fills the first free room (config order) that has an eligible token.
- **The nurse's no-show button** follows the grace rule: a first miss re-queues once and a second marks the no-show.
- **Wrapping numbers.** Token numbers wrap after 999 per clinic, department and day, skipping codes of active tokens: the contract allows 3 digits and 200/h passes 999 in a day.
- **Rooms config.** `config/rooms.yaml` adds `routing` (level to department) and a display `name` per room, which the board and `token.called` show.
- **The M1-05 port is not implemented in the API yet.** `QueueService.assign_token(db, …) -> TokenAssignment(token_id, token_code, department, room_hint, wait_estimate_min)` needs the caller's transaction, so M1-12 adapts the intake's `QueueService.assign` port with it; the API does not depend on the intake package yet.
- **Events.** `EventPublisher.add(db, event)` takes an `Event(type, data, timestamp)` checked against the contract model, rather than an envelope. `token.no_show` is new in the contract.
- **Simulation rooms.** The simulation's 66 rooms include 2 that take levels 1–2 only, to exercise room restrictions. All rooms accept levels 1–2, as the "never starved" check needs.
