# SHE-325: the nurse's worklist without one-by-one reads, and no screen going backwards

Status: 27 September 2026. A follow-up of M6-01 (latency), found by SHE-273's journey runs
(ADR-051). Research prototype; synthetic personas only; not for clinical use.

## What changed

- **One checkpoint read per visit.** `IntakeRuntime.view()` gives a visit's state and where it is
  paused from one checkpoint read.
- **One token query.** `QueueService.session_tokens()` finds every visit's token in one query,
  with `session_token`'s rule (which now uses it).
- **Concurrent worklist reads.** `GET /v1/nurse/worklist` reads the views concurrently (at most 8
  at a time) and the tokens once. Its output is unchanged.
- **The request's transaction commits before the response** (`deps.Db`, `scope="function"`). The
  queue routes (call next, arrived, no-show, seen) committed after their 200 was sent.
- **No screen goes backwards.** The worklist, the nurse's card and the phone's status show each
  read's answer as soon as it comes, and never an answer older than the one on screen. On the
  phone, a pushed status is newer than every read on its way.

## Found on the way

**The CI run of this change failed the "I feel worse" journey.** Its clean-up pressed "Seen (free
the room)"; the worklist still showed the button 20 s later (Expected 1, Received 2). Here it failed
once in four runs.

Two causes, both older than this change, which made them likelier:
1. **The commit came after the response.** A probe on a real uvicorn server showed FastAPI runs a
   `yield` dependency's exit after the client has the response (default scope), and before it
   with `scope="function"`. The queue routes' commit was that exit.
2. **The last answer won.** Overlapping reads of the worklist applied whichever answer came last,
   so an older read, started before the close, could put the closed patient back.

After the first fix alone, the journey still failed once in four runs (Expected 0, Received 1);
the second fix is the ordering. A first version of the ordering fix showed only the newest
read's answer. That held back fast answers: the chest-pain alert went to 2,114 to 2,252 ms and
failed its budget in three runs of five. The version kept shows every answer at once and drops
only older ones.
